# Design for Manufacturability (DFM) Final Project

This project centered on transforming an initial product concept into a fully manufacturable design through systematic engineering analysis, cost modeling, and iterative refinement. Completed as part of the Design for Manufacturability course, the project emphasized real‑world constraints such as tooling feasibility, part tolerances, assembly efficiency, and production cost targets. Working within a team, we evaluated the product’s geometry, materials, and manufacturing processes to ensure it could be produced reliably at scale. The final deliverable included a comprehensive engineering report, detailed CAD models, tolerance analyses, and cost breakdowns that demonstrated the product’s viability for mass production.

## Key Skills Applied

* **DFM Principles**: Applied core concepts including draft angles, parting lines, wall thickness, and mold flow considerations.
* **CAD Modeling**: Utilized advanced SolidWorks modeling for complex part geometry and assembly structure.
* **Tolerance Analysis**: Performed tolerance stack-ups to ensure reliable fit, function, and assembly robustness.
* **Process Selection**: Evaluated manufacturing processes (injection molding, machining, assembly methods) based on cost and feasibility.
* **Cost Modeling**: Conducted cost modeling and estimation using industry‑standard tools to evaluate production viability.
* **Engineering Communication**: Documented design decisions through formal reporting, technical drawings, and presentations.
* **Team Collaboration**: Integrated mechanical design, manufacturability constraints, and business considerations within a team environment.

## Project Summary

Throughout the semester, our team iteratively refined the product to reduce manufacturing complexity, improve part quality, and minimize production cost. We applied DFM concepts weekly, adjusting geometry, materials, and assembly strategy based on instructor feedback and manufacturability analysis. The final report documented the complete design evolution, including CAD revisions, tolerance stacks, cost drivers, and recommendations for scalable production.
